# Practical examples

[Back to overview](../README.md)

These examples illustrate workflows supported in the inspected source; they do not claim a specific legal outcome, guarantee a correct AI interpretation, or imply attorney-client representation.

### 1. Build a chronology from mixed correspondence

A user creates a case file, uploads letters and notices, extracts possible dates, reviews OCR where needed, and accepts the relevant events into a timeline. The result is a source-linked chronology for discussion with counsel.

### 2. Research a jurisdiction-specific question

The user selects a state and court context, asks a question in Source-Backed mode, opens retrieved authorities, reviews citation and coverage indicators, and exports a memorandum that includes applicable statutory-coverage warnings.

### 3. Organize disputed evidence

Uploaded records yield candidate facts; each can retain source wording, confidence, verification, and links to supporting documents. Separate legal issues list what still needs research. A disputed statement is not automatically promoted to a verified fact.

### 4. Prepare for the first attorney consultation

The user selects relevant documents, facts, issues, timeline entries and research, creates an attorney-preparation packet, and checks names, citations and evidence gaps before sending or printing the PDF/DOCX materials.

### 5. Review several records as one matter

A user keeps documents in one case file, runs multi-document synthesis, compares the extracted issues and dates with original sources, then produces a case report. Larger cases may exceed bounded excerpts; omissions should be checked manually.
